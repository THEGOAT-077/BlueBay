BlueBay: where accessible parking is missing

A crowd-sourced, real-time map of disabled parking bays, plus a Microburbs-powered score showing which City of Sydney suburbs need more of them. Team BlueBay, Microburbs Hackathon, 7 November 2026.

The problem

Drivers with a disability parking permit don’t know if a bay is free until they get there, and in many suburbs they don’t even know where the bays are. BlueBay answers both questions. People nearby confirm whether a bay is free or taken, drivers capture and release bays, and the app routes you to the nearest free one. This report covers the data step: where are accessible bays missing?

Data

  • Accessible bay locations: OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API, downloaded on 6 November 2026. We used parking_space=disabled bays, and car parks tagged with capacity:disabled above 0, excluding private ones. That gave 2,204 places in Greater Sydney, loaded into BlueBay’s Supabase database. Users can add more in the app.
  • Microburbs API (City of Sydney, 29 suburbs), called live through a Supabase Edge Function so the team key never reaches the browser or this repository:
    • /suburbs/{name}/shapes/boundary: suburb polygons, used to place each bay in a suburb
    • /demographics/age and /demographics/population-history: residents aged 55+ (the oldest bracket the API gives)
    • /lifestyle/pois: destinations people drive to, and health places in particular
    • /risks/slope: share of homes on steep ground (over 10°)
    • /lifestyle/landmarks: nearest hospital (shown in the app)
No Microburbs output is stored in this repository. The table below shows only our derived scores and our own bay counts.

Method: the clever step

Need for accessible parking isn’t just people. It’s older residents who are more likely to hold a permit, and the places everyone drives to, especially health services. Hills matter too: on a steep street, a bay 200 m away might as well not exist for a wheelchair user.

  1. Point in polygon: each mapped bay is placed in a suburb using the Microburbs boundaries.
  2. Demand index (0 to 1): the average of each suburb’s percentile rank on three signals, (a) residents aged 55+, (b) health places and (c) all destinations. Percentile ranks keep any one signal from dominating, since the CBD has 16,000 destinations.
  3. Hill factor: 1 + share of homes on steep ground.
  4. Gap score: demand × hill ÷ (mapped bays + 1). High demand, hills and few bays rank highest.

The same calculation runs live in the app (Where are bays missing?), using the bays currently in BlueBay’s database, so the ranking improves as people add bays.

Held-out checks

1. Does need explain where bays are mapped? We used leave-one-out cross-validation: fit a line predicting each suburb’s bay count from its demand index using the other 28 suburbs, then predict the held-out one.

The demand model does no better than the baseline, and the rank correlation is only 0.11. Need does not predict where accessible bays are mapped. Mapped supply follows stadiums and parks, not people. That gap is what BlueBay sets out to close.

2. Is the ranking robust? We recomputed the gap score three times, each time leaving one of the three demand signals out. The top 5 suburbs were identical every time: Surry Hills, Glebe, Darlinghurst, Potts Point and Ultimo, in a slightly different order. No single signal drives the result.

What worked

  • Joining OpenStreetMap bays to Microburbs suburb boundaries was quick and exact with point-in-polygon.
  • Percentile ranks gave a demand index that’s robust (check 2) and easy to explain.
  • Calling Microburbs through a Supabase Edge Function kept the key secret and put live area facts into the app: hills, older residents, health places and the nearest hospital.
  • The app itself: real-time capture, release and Free/Taken reports, GPS-verified actions, accounts, votes and badges, with the rules enforced in the database.

What did not work, and limits

  • OpenStreetMap is incomplete. The City of Sydney has far more accessible bays than the 55 mapped. So the gap score measures mapped supply, which partly reflects where volunteers have mapped. That’s exactly why BlueBay lets users add bays and rewards them with points and badges. The suburbs ranked highest are where we would ask people to map first.
  • The age data stops at 55+, so older residents is only a rough proxy for permit holders. Permit data by suburb would be better.
  • The demand index weights its three signals equally. With labelled data (for example, bay occupancy from BlueBay reports over time), the weights could be learned.
  • The live ranking takes about 12 seconds the first time, while it fetches 29 suburbs. After that it’s cached.
  • GPS checks stop casual cheating but not a spoofed phone location.

Next

  • Add City of Sydney open data on mobility parking to fill the mapping gap, and compare it with OpenStreetMap.
  • Use BlueBay’s own Free/Taken reports to measure real occupancy, then rank suburbs by bays that are always full, not just missing.
  • Show the gap as a map layer, and send “help map this suburb” prompts to users in the top-ranked suburbs.
